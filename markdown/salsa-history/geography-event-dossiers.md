# Salsa History Geography And Country Event Dossiers

Purpose: review country, city, migration, and geopolitical events that may help explain why salsa history moves through Cuba, Puerto Rico, New York, Colombia, Venezuela, Africa, and broader Latin America.

Implementation gate: do not add these items to `data/timeline*.ts` until Jake reviews this packet.

Timeline item specificity rule: a final public timeline item should anchor to a specific treaty, law, diplomatic action, performance, festival, recording, release, migration-policy event, or place-defining cultural milestone. Broad ranges are background buckets unless research can convert them into one or more specific candidates.

## How To Review

For each candidate, change `Review status: UNMARKED` to one of:

- `APPROVED`: good candidate for the public salsa-history timeline.
- `REJECT`: do not use publicly.
- `MAYBE`: needs more research or a better story angle.
- `MERGED`: fold into another public card.
- `LEDGER ONLY`: keep in research notes, not the public timeline.

- Type: geopolitical / social-history event.
- Place: Puerto Rico.
- Review status: LEDGER ONLY
- Recommended action: LEDGER ONLY unless a future roots chapter explicitly foregrounds slavery, abolition, and Afro-Caribbean musical survival.

### Migration And New York Formation

#### 1929 - Anti-Machado crisis strengthens Cuban exile/migrant networks in New York

- Type: migration / political-exile context.
- Place: Cuba; New York City.
- Review status: MAYBE
- Recommended action: MERGED into Bauza/Machito/New York bridge context, or LEDGER ONLY.
- Proposed timeline wording: Political and economic pressure under Gerardo Machado pushes more Cuban migrants and exiles toward New York, strengthening the Cuban musical presence that bridge figures like Mario Bauza would move through.
- Verified claims: NPS says many Cuban migrants came in 1929 fleeing economic and political effects of Machado's government; NPS also notes Cuban and Puerto Rican social clubs in New York hosted musical performances.

- Uncertainty: NPS supports the year and broad migration context, but a public card would need more specific music-scene evidence.

#### 1947 - Operation Bootstrap accelerates Puerto Rico-to-mainland migration pressures

- Type: economic-policy / migration context.
- Place: Puerto Rico; New York City.
- Review status: MAYBE
- Recommended action: MAYBE enrichment for a Puerto Rico/New York migration card.

### Cuba-U.S. Rupture

#### 1959-01-01 - Cuban Revolution begins a new exile and music-exchange era

- Type: revolution / migration-and-industry turning point.
- Place: Havana; Cuba; United States.
- Review status: APPROVED
- Recommended action: APPROVED candidate, probably paired with the 1961/1962 rupture in one public geography card.
- Proposed timeline wording: The Cuban Revolution transforms migration, exile, media industries, and Cuba-U.S. cultural exchange, helping shift salsa's later commercial center toward New York and Puerto Rican networks while Cuban music continues to influence from both island and diaspora.
- Verified claims: NPS dates Castro's revolutionary victory to 1959; NPS says many Cubans came to the United States after the revolution; NPS also notes that Cuban musicians such as Los Van Van and Irakere continued inside Cuba while others, including Celia Cruz and Arsenio Rodriguez, made New York their musical home.
